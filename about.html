01

The problem

RESET mining has a hard floor: a wallet must lock 0.1 RESET before the Mine accepts a claim. Below that, the protocol rejects the claim entirely — no partial payout, nothing. The floor exists to keep dust wallets from crowding the claim lanes, and it works.

But it also means someone holding 0.02 RESET cannot mine at all. Selling that position to reach 0.1 defeats the purpose of holding it. This pool fixes the access problem without touching the protocol's rules.

Pooling buys access, never extra yield. Anyone claiming otherwise is selling you something. Design principle

The Mine's fair share is strictly linear in deposit. Thirty people pooling 0.1 earn exactly what one person with 0.1 earns. This pool makes no claim to the contrary — its value is access, automation, and auditability, never inflated yield.

02

How it works

A member registers their RESET address and proves ownership by signing a challenge with their wallet key. They deposit to the pool wallet; the operator verifies the transfer on-chain and credits their principal in the ledger. The operator then locks the combined pool into the Mine and claims every round at level 1000 — exactly the fair share, never more, so the pool never accrues strikes or penalties.

When a round settles, the payout is split pro-rata by principal snapshot taken at claim time. Integer arithmetic only. The rounding remainder is tracked in a dedicated dust counter and never silently absorbed. Conservation is enforced by construction: every unit of a round's payout lands as member net, fee, or dust.

⏱
Auto-Stake — Your Choice
Each member owns an autoStake flag. ON: earnings compound into your principal automatically every round. OFF: earnings accumulate as a withdrawable balance. Toggle any time, effective next settle. The operator cannot set it for you.
⭐
Stakers
Whitelisted capital providers with ≥ 0.5 RESET principal pay 0% fee on their own earnings and receive 50% of the round's collected fees, split pro-rata. Below 0.5 RESET, a flagged member is an ordinary member that round.
⏳
Exits
Earnings withdraw at ≥ 0.01 RESET — no lock. Principal exits follow the protocol's own schedule: unlock, 144 counted blocks (~24 h), then withdraw. Exits are batched by the operator to minimize network fees.
03

Trust & safety

The contract is the ledger. The operator runs the loop. Only one of them can steal. Custody model, stated plainly

Every parameter is written once at pool_init and nothing can change it. Fee tiers, minimums, staker share — all frozen. The only setter ops are set_staker (operator whitelist), set_fee_receivers (once, then locked forever), and set_auto_stake (each member, own record only).

Deposits are idempotent by transfer reference. A round cannot be double-settled. A sweep cannot exceed what has actually accrued. Principal and earnings can only move to their owner. Every rejected operation leaves the ledger state byte-identical — verified by an adversarial harness driving the real contract sources.

Every member holds a replicated copy of the ledger. The pool wallet address is public and its balance is readable from the gateway by anyone. A read-only solvency check compares what the contract owes against what the wallet actually holds. If those numbers ever diverge, every member sees it at once.

The honest boundary: RESET has no multisig. The operator holds the pool wallet keys and signs real transfers. A dishonest operator is detectable and attributable — not prevented. That is the accurate description of the trust model. Start with an amount you would not mind losing, and reconcile the ledger yourself.
